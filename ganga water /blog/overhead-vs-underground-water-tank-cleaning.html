Overhead vs Underground Water Tank Cleaning: What Should You Know?

Aug 2025 · 9 min read · Tank Solutions

Understanding Your Water Storage System

Most properties in cities like Hyderabad utilize a dual water storage system: an underground sump that receives municipal water and an overhead tank that distributes it via gravity to the rest of the building. While they both store water, overhead and underground tanks face distinctly different environmental challenges and require different professional cleaning approaches.

Overhead Tank Cleaning: Key Considerations

Overhead tanks are typically placed on rooftops, exposing them to direct sunlight, extreme heat, and airborne pollutants. Here are the key challenges when cleaning them:

  • Access and Safety: Because they are elevated, cleaning them requires proper safety gear to prevent falls. Narrow access points on rooftops can also make maneuverability difficult.
  • Common Contaminants: Being outdoors, overhead tanks are highly prone to algae growth (due to sunlight penetration), dust, and occasionally bird droppings or insects if the lid is improperly sealed. UV degradation can also affect plastic tanks.
  • High-Pressure Process: Cleaning usually requires robust high-pressure washing to scrub off dried-on scale and green algae from the inner walls.
  • Frequency: We recommend every 6 months to maintain optimal water quality.

Underground Tank / Sump Cleaning: Key Considerations

Underground tanks or sumps serve as the primary receiving basin for municipal or tanker water. They have entirely different requirements:

  • Confined Space Safety: Cleaning an underground sump requires entering a confined space. This demands specialized safety protocols, adequate ventilation, and specialized breathing equipment in certain cases to prevent asphyxiation from accumulated gases.
  • Sediment Accumulation: Sumps endure the heaviest load of sand, silt, and dirt that comes directly from municipal supply lines. The sludge layer at the bottom can be significantly thicker and heavier than in overhead tanks.
  • Emptying Process: Removing water from a deep underground tank requires powerful submersible sludge pumps that DIY efforts typically lack.
  • Biofilm and Slime: The cool, dark environment is perfect for bacterial biofilms and slime to develop on the walls, requiring intense scrubbing and chemical disinfection.
  • Frequency: Typically every 6 to 12 months.

Which Needs More Frequent Cleaning?

Neither is entirely immune to contamination, but they degrade in different ways. Overhead tanks are exposed to sunlight, promoting rapid algae growth. Underground sumps are the first point of contact for municipal water, making them the primary collection point for heavy sediment and silt. As best practice, they should ideally be cleaned simultaneously to ensure the entire water system in the house remains uncontaminated.

The Cleaning Process: What to Expect

When you hire professionals, the approaches will differ slightly:

  • Overhead: Our team arrives with high-pressure washers, sets up safety harnesses if required, pumps out the remaining water, scrubs the tank walls to remove algae, vacuums the remaining water, and finally disinfects the interior.
  • Underground: The team deploys heavy-duty submersible pumps to dewater the sump, safely enters the confined space, manually scrubs heavy slime, vacuums out thick sludge from the floor, and applies an hygiene UV or chemical treatment.

Why Professional Cleaning Matters for Both

Attempting to clean either tank type yourself carries risks. For overhead tanks, there is a severe risk of slips and falls. For underground sumps, the confined space poses serious breathing hazards. Additionally, without commercial-grade high-pressure washers and vacuums, DIY cleaning usually just stirs up the sludge without removing the microscopic biofilms. Professional equipment ensures a thorough, hygienic outcome.

Maintaining Both Tanks Between Cleanings

To extend the life of your clean tanks, homeowners should always ensure that lids are tightly secured to prevent dust, insects, and light penetration. It is also beneficial to check for visible cracks or leaks periodically.
